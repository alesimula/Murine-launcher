# Superseded monogram artwork

This direction was replaced by the dedicated launcher home-and-grid symbol.

## Generation

Both masters were created with the built-in image generation tool. The original blog logo was used as a visual reference for the new mark; the new mark was then used as the cover reference. The SVG and Android vectors are export assets from the generated master, not additional generated images.

Logo prompt:

> Create the final standalone logo mark for an Android launcher named r4l.launcher, in the personal brand family of the provided R4L blog logo. Reference image is STYLE AND IDENTITY REFERENCE, not an edit target. Keep the angular, forward-leaning interlocking R4L monogram idea but simplify into bold flat geometric vector-like forms readable at app-icon sizes. Off-white #EDF7FF main strokes, electric violet #6554F4 accent on the 4 / upward launch chevron, deep charcoal #1D242B internal negative space and very small outline. Crisp edges, balanced optically, no tiny highlights, no gradients, no chrome or 3D. Single centered compact monogram, roughly square, generous empty transparent margin. It must read R4L. Only the R4L mark; no additional text, no wordmark, no device, no scenery, no shadow, no grid or presentation sheet. Genuine transparent background. This is a new sibling logo adapted for launcher use.

Cover prompt:

> Generate a wide landscape brand cover image for r4l.launcher, a personal Android launcher project by Rafael Maciel, to fit the visual style of his R4L blog. Use the attached new logo as the EXACT brand mark identity reference. Composition: carefully designed editorial software project cover, dark charcoal #1D242B matte background with restrained electric violet #6554F4 geometric diagonals and subtle low contrast grid. Large sharp R4L mark on the left, off-white and violet, matching reference. On the right the exact lowercase wordmark 'r4l.launcher' in chunky rounded arcade-inspired typography consistent with Bungee, with a violet dot between r4l and launcher. A small clean caption below: 'Your home. Your categories.' A minimal abstract outline of Android app category tiles at bottom right, subtle, not an actual screenshot, no fabricated UI. Flat graphic design, confident and spacious, crisp white type, no chrome, no 3D mockup, no stock devices, no mascots, no extra text. Wide 3:2 or 16:9 image, strong negative space and generous margins. This must feel like a sibling of a charcoal / off-white / violet developer blog with bold Bungee headings and an angular gaming monogram. Final production brand artwork, not a style board.
